Edit a PDF without retyping it

PDFs are great for sharing but awkward to change. Converting a PDF to Word gives you a normal .docx file you can edit in Microsoft Word, Google Docs, Apple Pages or LibreOffice. Fix a typo in a letter, update last year's form, or reuse a few paragraphs from a report, without copying and pasting line by line.

How to convert PDF to Word with SwapDoc

1

Drop your PDF in the box above. Or click the box to choose it. Files up to 25 MB are supported.

2

Click Convert to Word. SwapDoc rebuilds the pages as an editable Word document. This usually takes a few seconds, longer for long documents.

3

Open and tidy up. Open the .docx in Word and check the layout. Simple documents are usually ready to go; complex ones may need a few minutes of tidying.

What we tested, and what came out

Tested on {{ TESTED_LABEL }} on the live converter.

A PDF doesn't really contain paragraphs, headings or tables. It records where each line and picture sits on the page, and the converter has to rebuild a document from that. We converted three kinds of PDF to see how well it does:

  • a 3-page PDF made from Word, with a table, a picture, a link, a landscape page and several fonts and languages;
  • a two-column newsletter;
  • a scan of the same 3 pages, saved as pictures.

What that means for you

Good for: changing a few words, a date, a name or a figure while keeping the layout exactly as it was, such as a form, a letter or a price list. Click into a line and type.

Awkward for: rewriting whole paragraphs, because text won't flow from one line to the next. For that, either:

  • select all (Ctrl + A), copy, and paste into a new document with Paste → Keep Text Only, then re-apply headings; or
  • open the PDF directly in Microsoft Word (File → Open), which rebuilds flowing paragraphs and real tables but is less faithful to the original layout.

Scans need text recognition (OCR), which SwapDoc doesn't do. Google Docs does it for free; see our guide to turning a PDF into an editable Word document. For more clean-up tricks, read fixing formatting after converting.

PDF to Word questions

Will the Word document look exactly like the PDF?
It looks the same, page for page, because each line is placed exactly where it was. The trade-off is that each line sits in its own text box, so it's ideal for small edits but awkward for rewriting paragraphs.
Can I convert a scanned PDF?
It will convert, but into a Word file containing a picture of each page rather than editable text, because SwapDoc doesn't do text recognition (OCR).
Can I convert a password-protected PDF?
No. Remove the password first in the program you normally open it with, if you're allowed to, then convert the unlocked copy.
What format is the Word file?
You get a .docx file, which opens in Microsoft Word 2007 or later, Google Docs, Apple Pages and LibreOffice.
