Edit a PDF without retyping it

PDFs are great for sharing but awkward to change. Converting a PDF to Word gives you a normal .docx file you can edit in Microsoft Word, Google Docs, Apple Pages or LibreOffice. Fix a typo in a letter, update last year's form, or reuse a few paragraphs from a report, without copying and pasting line by line.

What to expect

A PDF doesn't really contain paragraphs, headings or tables. It records where each line and image sits on the page. To make an editable document, the converter has to work out the structure again, so results depend on how the PDF was made.

Converts well

PDFs saved from Word or another word processor: letters, reports, essays and CVs with mostly plain text.

May need tidying

Magazine-style layouts, multiple columns, complex tables and designed brochures. These often come back with text in separate boxes. Our guide to fixing formatting after converting explains how to clean them up quickly.

Won't become editable text

Scanned documents and photos of pages. These are pictures of text, so the Word file will contain the picture. To get editable text you need OCR (text recognition). Our guide How to turn a PDF into an editable Word document shows a free way to do that.

PDF to Word questions

Will the Word document look exactly like the PDF?
Usually close, but not always identical. Simple text documents convert well; complex layouts may come back as text boxes that need tidying.
Can I convert a scanned PDF?
It will convert, but into a Word file containing a picture of each page rather than editable text, because SwapDoc doesn't do text recognition (OCR).
Can I convert a password-protected PDF?
No. Remove the password first in the program you normally open it with, if you're allowed to, then convert the unlocked copy.
What format is the Word file?
You get a .docx file, which opens in Microsoft Word 2007 or later, Google Docs, Apple Pages and LibreOffice.
