Quick answer: a scanned PDF is a picture of text, so a normal PDF to Word converter gives you pictures back. You need OCR (text recognition). The easiest free option is Google Docs: upload the PDF to Google Drive, then right-click → Open with → Google Docs.

Is your PDF scanned?

Open it and try either of these:

  • Select a word by dragging over it. If the whole page highlights as one block, or nothing highlights, it's a picture.
  • Search with Ctrl + F (Cmd + F on a Mac) for a word you can see. If it isn't found, the text isn't real text.

Scans come from scanners, phone scanning apps and "scan to email" office printers. PDFs saved from Word, websites and most online forms contain real text and convert directly with our PDF to Word converter.

What happens if you convert a scan without OCR

We tested this. We turned a 3-page document into a scanned PDF and converted it with SwapDoc's PDF to Word converter. The Word file had a picture of each page and no editable text at all. That's what any converter without OCR will do. Use one of the methods below instead.

1. Google Docs (any computer, free)

  1. Go to drive.google.com and sign in with a Google account.
  2. Click New → File upload and choose your PDF.
  3. Right-click the uploaded file and choose Open with → Google Docs.
  4. A new document opens with the recognised text below an image of each page. Delete the images if you don't need them.
  5. To get a Word file: File → Download → Microsoft Word (.docx).

Google says this works best on files of 2 MB or less, with clear text in common fonts. It keeps bold, italics and paragraphs reasonably well, but tables and columns usually need re-building.

2. Microsoft OneNote (Windows, free)

  1. Save the scanned page as a picture, or take a screenshot of it. Then, in OneNote, use Insert → Pictures to add it to a page.
  2. Right-click the picture and choose Copy Text from Picture.
  3. Paste into Word.

Good for a few pages at a time.

3. Mac Preview and Live Text (Mac, iPhone, iPad)

Recent versions of macOS and iOS recognise text in pictures automatically. Open the scan in Preview, hover over the text, and drag to select it as if it were normal text. Copy and paste into your document. On an iPhone, open the PDF or photo, press and hold on the text, and choose Copy.

4. Windows Snipping Tool (Windows 11)

Open the scanned page on screen, press Windows + Shift + S and snip the area. Open the snip in Snipping Tool and click Text actions to select and copy the text. Quick for a paragraph or a table cell.

5. Google Lens (Android)

Point the camera at the paper, or open a screenshot in Google Photos, and tap Lens → Text to select and copy. Handy for short documents you still have on paper.

Getting the best results

  • Scan at 300 dpi, in black and white or greyscale, for text documents.
  • Keep the page straight and flat. Curled or skewed lines confuse OCR.
  • Good contrast: dark text on a clean background. Faded print and coloured paper reduce accuracy.
  • Proofread, especially numbers, names and punctuation. OCR commonly confuses 0 and O, 1 and l, and rn and m.
  • Handwriting is much less reliable than print with any free tool.

Keep private documents private

Bank statements, medical letters and ID documents are best kept on your own device: Live Text on a Mac or iPhone and Snipping Tool on Windows work without uploading the file to a new service.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if a PDF is scanned?
If you can't select individual words, or a search finds nothing, it's a scan.
What is OCR?
Optical character recognition: software that reads the letters in a picture and turns them into editable text.
What is the best free way to convert a scanned PDF to Word?
For most people, Google Docs: upload to Drive, then Open with → Google Docs, then download as .docx.
Why does converting a scanned PDF to Word give me pictures?
Because the scan is a picture. Converters without OCR keep it as one.
