Quick answer: big PDFs are nearly always big because of pictures. The best fix is to shrink the pictures in the original document and make the PDF again. If you only have the PDF, a Mac can compress it with Preview, and on Windows you can re-save it through a free PDF tool.

What's making it big?

Text costs almost nothing: a 20-page report of plain text is typically well under 1 MB. The usual culprits are:

  • Photos straight from a phone or camera. A modern phone photo is often 3 to 5 MB on its own, at a resolution far higher than a printed page needs.
  • Scanned pages, especially in colour at high resolution. Every page is a picture.
  • Screenshots pasted in as PNG images, which can be large.
  • Many embedded fonts, usually a small part of the total.

Best fix: shrink the pictures before making the PDF

If you have the Word document, fix it there. You keep control over quality, and the text stays perfectly sharp.

  1. Click any picture, then Picture Format → Compress Pictures.
  2. Untick Apply only to this picture so it affects all of them.
  3. Tick Delete cropped areas of pictures.
  4. Choose a resolution: 150 ppi (Web) is fine for documents that will be read on screen; 220 ppi (Print) for documents that will be printed.
  5. Click OK, save, then make the PDF.

Then choose "Minimum size" when saving

In Word for Windows, go to File → Save As, choose PDF as the file type, then under "Optimize for" pick Minimum size (publishing online) and save. This reduces picture quality further. Use Standard if the document will be printed.

You can also convert the slimmed Word file with our Word to PDF converter, which keeps your pictures at the size they are in the document.

Only have the PDF?

On a Mac: Preview

  1. Open the PDF in Preview.
  2. Choose File → Export.
  3. Set Quartz Filter to Reduce File Size and save it under a new name.

This is aggressive: it can shrink a file enormously but make photos blurry. Always open the result and check it.

On Windows

Windows has no built-in PDF compressor. Options:

  • Convert it back to Word and re-save. Convert the PDF with our PDF to Word converter, compress the pictures as above, and make a new PDF. This suits text documents best; the layout may need a little tidying.
  • A free PDF tool with a "compress" option, such as the free desktop app PDF24 Creator or Adobe's free online Compress PDF tool. Read the privacy terms of any online tool before uploading anything confidential.

For scanned documents

If you can, scan again with better settings: greyscale or black and white instead of colour, at 200 to 300 dpi. A black-and-white text page at 300 dpi is usually a fraction of the size of the same page in colour. Phone scanning apps often have a "document" or "black and white" mode that does this.

How small does it need to be?

Where it's goingTypical limit
Gmail attachment25 MB
Most other email servicesAround 20 MB
Job sites and application formsOften 2 to 5 MB
Council, school and government formsOften 5 to 10 MB
SwapDoc uploads25 MB

These limits vary and change, so check the page you're uploading to. For anything too big to email, a shared link from OneDrive, Google Drive or iCloud is often easier than compressing.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my PDF so large?
Pictures: full-size photos or high-resolution colour scans.
How do I make a PDF smaller in Word?
Compress the pictures, then Save As PDF with "Minimum size (publishing online)".
How do I reduce a PDF's size on a Mac for free?
Preview → File → Export → Quartz Filter: Reduce File Size.
What size should a PDF be for email?
Under 20 MB for most email, but under 2 to 5 MB for many upload forms.
