PDFs are designed to be read, not edited. When you need to change one, the easiest fix is usually to turn it into a Word document, edit it, and save a new PDF. Here are three free ways to do that, and how to handle the tricky cases.

First: what kind of PDF do you have?

Open the PDF and try to select a word with your mouse.

  • You can highlight individual words: it's a text PDF, usually made from Word or another program. It will convert into editable text.
  • The whole page highlights as one block, or nothing happens: it's a scanned PDF or a photo of a page. You'll need text recognition (OCR) to get editable text; see method 3.

1. Use an online converter

  1. Go to SwapDoc's PDF to Word converter.
  2. Drop your PDF onto the box, or click to choose it.
  3. Click Convert to Word and open the .docx that downloads.

This works on any device and doesn't need Word installed to do the conversion. Your file is deleted from the server once your download finishes.

2. Open the PDF directly in Microsoft Word

Word 2013 and later (including Microsoft 365) can open PDFs and turn them into editable documents on your own computer.

  1. In Word, go to File → Open and choose the PDF. You may need to set the file type to All files or PDF files to see it.
  2. Word warns that it will convert the PDF into an editable document and that it may not look exactly like the original. Click OK.
  3. Edit the document, then use File → Save As to save it as a Word document, or as a new PDF.

3. Use Google Docs for scanned PDFs

Google Drive can run text recognition on scanned PDFs and images for free.

  1. Upload the PDF to Google Drive.
  2. Right-click it and choose Open with → Google Docs.
  3. Google creates a new document with the recognised text, usually below an image of the original page. Check it carefully: OCR often confuses similar characters, like 0 and O or 1 and l.
  4. Go to File → Download → Microsoft Word (.docx).

Text recognition works best on clear, straight, high-contrast scans. Handwriting, faint photocopies and photos taken at an angle give poor results.

Why the layout sometimes changes

A PDF records exactly where each line of text and each image sits on the page. It doesn't know that some lines form a paragraph, that a row of text is a table, or that a block is a heading. A converter has to guess that structure back. When it can't be sure, it keeps the look by putting text in separate boxes, which looks right but is fiddly to edit.

The cleaner the original PDF, the better the result. If you get a messy conversion, our guide to fixing formatting after converting covers the usual problems.

Quick tips

  • Only need a few lines? Copying text straight from the PDF and pasting with Paste → Keep Text Only in Word is often quicker than converting the whole thing.
  • Need to fill in a form? Many PDF forms can be filled in directly in a free PDF reader or your web browser, without converting.
  • Password-protected PDFs can't be converted until the password is removed, and only do that if you have permission.
  • Respect copyright. Converting lets you edit a document; it doesn't give you the right to reuse someone else's work.
