Quick answer: send a PDF unless you're asked for Word. A PDF looks the same on every screen and printer, so your layout, fonts and page count stay exactly as you designed them. Send Word when an advert, form or recruitment agency asks for it, because they may need to edit it.

When to send a PDF

  • Applying directly to an employer by email or through their website.
  • Your CV has a designed layout, with a particular font, columns, a sidebar or a strict two-page limit.
  • You've used a font others may not have, such as Aptos (Word's new default) or anything downloaded. In a PDF, the font travels with the file.
  • You want to stop accidental changes. It's easy to nudge a Word file while reading it; a PDF stays put.

When to send Word

  • The advert or application form asks for .doc or .docx. Always follow the instructions. Some older recruitment systems only accept Word.
  • A recruitment agency asks for it. Agencies commonly remove your contact details and add their own branding before sending your CV to the employer.
  • Someone is helping you edit it, such as a careers adviser, mentor or friend.

If you send Word, use a safe font such as Calibri or Arial and check how it looks in another app, such as Google Docs, before sending. If a form only accepts the old .doc format, our DOC ⇄ DOCX converter turns a .docx into one.

What about applicant tracking systems?

Many employers use an applicant tracking system (ATS) to collect and search applications. A modern ATS reads PDFs made from Word without trouble. What causes problems is the way the CV is built, not the format:

  • Scanned or photographed CVs contain no real text. The system sees a picture.
  • Text inside images, text boxes or shapes may be skipped.
  • Complex columns and tables can be read in the wrong order, mixing your job titles with your skills list.
  • Contact details in the header are ignored by some systems. Put your name, phone and email in the main body.
  • Unusual headings such as "My journey" may not be recognised. Stick to "Experience", "Education" and "Skills".

The copy-and-paste test

Open your PDF, press Ctrl + A (Cmd + A on a Mac) to select everything, copy, and paste into Notepad or TextEdit. If the text comes out in a sensible order with nothing missing, a tracking system can read it. If sections are jumbled or missing, simplify the layout.

Checklist before you send

  1. Make the PDF in Word with File → Save As → PDF, so your fonts are embedded. Our Word to PDF converter also works, and our tests show exactly which fonts it keeps.
  2. Remove tracked changes and comments left over from anyone who reviewed it. See removing tracked changes before making a PDF.
  3. Check the page count in the PDF matches your Word document.
  4. Click every link, such as your email address, LinkedIn and portfolio.
  5. Name the file properly: Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf.
  6. Keep it small: a text CV should be well under 1 MB. If it's bigger, a large photo or logo is usually the cause.
  7. Do the copy-and-paste test above.

A note on UK CVs

UK employers generally don't expect a photo, date of birth or marital status on a CV, and leaving them out reduces the risk of unconscious bias. Two pages is the usual length for experienced candidates, one page for school leavers and graduates. Always check the advert, as some sectors, such as academia and medicine, have their own conventions.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to send a CV as a PDF or Word document?
PDF, unless you're asked for Word.
Can applicant tracking systems read PDF CVs?
Yes, if the PDF contains real text. Avoid scans, text in images and complex columns.
What should I name my CV file?
Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf.
How do I check my CV can be read by a computer?
Copy all the text from the PDF and paste it into Notepad or TextEdit. It should read in a sensible order.
