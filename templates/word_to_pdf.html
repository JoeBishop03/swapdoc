Why turn a Word document into a PDF?

A Word document can look different on someone else's computer. If they don't have the same fonts, or open it in a different version of Word, Google Docs or Pages, lines can wrap differently, pictures can move and a two-page CV can spill onto a third. A PDF fixes the layout in place, so it looks the same everywhere and prints exactly as you intended.

PDFs are also harder to change by accident, open on almost any device without Word, and are what most job sites, councils, schools and online forms ask for when you upload a document.

Tips for a perfect PDF

Use common fonts

Calibri, Cambria, Arial, Times New Roman and Courier New come out identical to Word. Newer documents use Aptos, which converts to a very similar font; check that nothing has moved onto an extra page.

Accept or reject tracked changes first

In our test, tracked changes appeared in the PDF as coloured, underlined and struck-through text, which you rarely want in a finished document. In Word, go to Review → Accept → Accept All Changes (or reject the ones you don't want), then save and convert. Comments are left out of the PDF automatically. Our guide to removing tracked changes and comments has the steps for Word, Google Docs and Mac.

Use real headings

Headings made with Word's Heading styles (rather than just bold, larger text) make the document easier to read with a screen reader and easier to navigate.

Keep files under 25 MB

Large photos are the usual reason a document is big. In Word, select a picture, then Picture Format → Compress Pictures to shrink them before converting.

Word to PDF questions

Will my PDF look exactly like my Word document?
In most cases, yes. Text, images, tables, headers and footers are kept. Documents in Calibri, Cambria, Arial, Times New Roman or Courier New come out with identical line breaks. Other fonts, including Aptos, are swapped for a close match, which can move a line or two.
Will comments and tracked changes show in the PDF?
Comments are left out. Tracked changes are shown as coloured markup, so accept or reject them in Word before converting.
Can I convert an old .doc file?
Yes. Both .docx (Word 2007 and later) and the older .doc format (Word 97 to 2003) are supported. Not sure which you have? Read DOC vs DOCX.
Do links still work in the PDF?
Yes. Hyperlinks in your Word document stay clickable in the PDF.
Is a watermark added?
No. SwapDoc never adds watermarks, logos or extra pages.
Can I do this without uploading my file?
Yes. If you have Word, you can save as PDF on your own computer. Our guide How to convert Word to PDF shows how, along with other free options.
